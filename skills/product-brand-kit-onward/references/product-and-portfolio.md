# Product Readiness and Brand Hierarchy

For a new product, establish what the business can deliver and which name should build trust with customers. Readiness determines what the page can promise. Hierarchy determines whose name the customer sees first. Ask the product owner for evidence that changes either decision.

## Establish readiness from evidence

Use real samples, orders, trial records, and support data when they exist. A concept can still have a complete review kit. Use an accurate next step such as seeing a demo or registering interest while payment or fulfillment remains unavailable. A design assignment provides scope for design work. Get authorization for separate work such as actual customer interviews, collecting payments, or going live.

| Question | Evidence and decision | Effect on the kit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who has this problem? | Find a recent instance. Record who initiated, chose, paid, and used the product, and what they did before. Save the date and source. Confirm permission before quoting participants publicly. | Pick the audience, scene, and story opening. |
| Does the product change that moment? | Use the smallest sample that demonstrates the experience, such as a tasting, clickable flow, or manually delivered service. Define the behavior and success criteria before testing. Track interest, payment, and repeat use separately. | Explain how the product delivers value. Mark concepts, samples, and current versions accurately. |
| Can this be delivered consistently? | Confirm the price unit, costs, capacity, staffing, and the assumptions most likely to change the offer. Use a prototype or pilot for physical products, working features and support for digital ones, and actual booking capacity for services. | Keep internal costs and staffing in the brief. Publish verified price units, inclusions, and conditions. |
| Can a customer act today? | Walk through discover, choose, act, receive or activate, and get help. Check the sample, purchase or booking route, fulfillment conditions, and support answer. | Match the call to action to readiness. Use consistent prices, variants, regions, and lead times across the page and support copy. Track produced, approved, and published states separately. |
| What should change after launch? | Choose a metric close to the goal. Record the numerator, denominator, channel, period, and baseline. Compare support questions, drop-off, complaints, or refunds. Treat small samples as signals to investigate. | Change the point where readers are getting stuck. A confusing hero can need clearer copy. Paid orders that do not arrive need a fulfillment check. |

A useful experiment records the audience, expected action, test, measure, decision threshold, result, limits, and next change. For example, payment records show that someone accepted the offer in that channel. An efficacy claim still needs product evidence of its own. Existing results can be reused when they apply. Recheck them when the audience, channel, price, or core experience changes. ## Confirm the roles and names

| Record | Ask only what affects the credit, claim, or sales path |
| --- | --- |
| Legal and operating roles | Who owns or applies for the mark, who can use it in this market, and who sells, fulfills, or supports the offer? Who can authorize a co-brand, manufacturer, or reseller to use a name or logo and where? |
| Names customers see | How should the legal name, public parent brand, line, product, category, and variant be written? Which name is recognized and which stands behind the promise? |
| Business priority | Is the product a flagship, an entry into a new audience, an addition to a range, an add-on, or a limited trial? What launch resources and owner are committed? |
| Portfolio relationships | What does it replace, complement, fit, bundle with, or upgrade? How should customers choose between products? Might more versions, services, or channel exclusives use the same name? |

Support relationship and rights claims with the relevant authorization or registration. Use the actual selling and support entity wherever required for transactions.

## Decide launch emphasis and brand independence separately

| Situation | Name, mark, and story | Placement and relationship |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Regular addition for the same audience | Use the parent mark and a descriptive product name, with variant on its own line. Show the task the product serves. | Use existing range or store entry points and clarify how to choose. A different capacity, color, or flavor may just be a variant. |
| Flagship carrying the parent promise | Keep the parent recognizable. Give the flagship a stable full name and show a verified design choice that supports its story. | Give it prominent space, demos, retail displays, or a press story. Explain its place alongside entry and professional options. |
| A distinct line drawing on parent trust | Lead with line and category. Agree the parent's endorsement wording, placement, and authorization. Carry over compatible visual elements and give the line a recognizable cue. | Put the shared story on the line page and practical differences on product pages. Test first and repeated exposure in real placements. |
| A largely independent brand | Establish the case, durable resources, seller, rights, and owner. Give the brand its own name, mark, voice, and entry points. | Clarify how it differs from siblings in audience, price, and promise. A cross-sell should state what the other product adds. Confirm factual company relationships before claiming an endorsement. |
| Co-brand, license, accessory, or contract manufacture | Use wording that reflects the documented relationship, such as “in partnership with,” “compatible with,” or a manufacturer credit. Record order, scope, term, and approval. | Make clear who sells, makes, and supports the product, and what it works with. Resolve disputed descriptions before public use. |

A portable kettle can use a parent mark as a routine range extension, and use the same mark as a commuter flagship with a prominent homepage story and a demo. A distinct outdoor line raises a separate decision about an endorsed or independent identity. A real example of a documented hierarchy is available in [Red Hat's brand architecture](https://www.redhat.com/en/about/brand/standards/brand-architecture).

Hand over the readiness stage, campaign goal, portfolio role, naming structure, leading name, secondary credit and placement, differentiator, support owner, and evidence owner. Carry that decision into the brief, listings, sales copy, support, and PDF. Update the linked pieces if the role or relationships change.
